On November 3, 2026, 3M announced what may be the largest [personal injury](/how-much-does-personal-injury-lawyer-cost/) mass-tort settlement of the decade: a $7.35 billion deal to resolve nearly all claims tied to PFAS "forever chemicals" in aqueous film-forming foam, known as AFFF. That's the firefighting foam used for decades at military bases, airports, and fire training facilities across the country. If you or someone you know was regularly exposed to AFFF and later developed certain cancers or other serious conditions, this settlement directly affects you, and deadlines are already moving.

The scale here is hard to overstate. According to 3M's own announcement on November 3, the deal covers approximately 115,000 to 120,000 eligible plaintiffs, which represents roughly 99% of current [personal injury claims](/la-wildfire-personal-injury-claims-what-victims-can-recover/) both inside and outside the AFFF multidistrict litigation (MDL) consolidated in South Carolina. An MDL is a federal procedure that groups thousands of similar lawsuits before one judge to handle pretrial proceedings together, which is why so many AFFF cases ended up in the same court. The settlement's present value is approximately $5.8 billion, with the full $7.35 billion paid out in installments through 2034.

This isn't a done deal for everyone, though. And the payment timeline has some real quirks that [victims need](/nuclear-verdicts-are-rising-what-injury-victims-need-to-know/) to understand before they decide anything.

## Who Qualifies and What Injuries Are Covered

The settlement recognizes six specific medical conditions. That list matters enormously, because if your condition isn't on it, this settlement doesn't help you, at least not directly.

| Qualifying Injury | Category |
|---|---|
| Kidney cancer | Cancer |
| Testicular cancer | Cancer |
| Liver cancer | Cancer |
| Thyroid cancer | Cancer |
| Thyroid disease | Non-cancer condition |
| Ulcerative colitis | Non-cancer condition |

Notice that two of those six are non-cancer diagnoses. Thyroid disease and ulcerative colitis, a chronic inflammatory bowel condition, are included alongside the cancers. That's significant because it broadens the pool well beyond people with malignancies. If you were a firefighter, military veteran, or airport worker with heavy AFFF exposure and you've been diagnosed with any of these six conditions, you likely fall within the covered plaintiff group.

Exposure history matters too. The claims center on people who had substantial contact with AFFF specifically, not just general PFAS exposure from drinking water or consumer products. If your exposure came primarily from contaminated groundwater near a military base rather than direct foam contact, your claim profile may look different. Talk to an attorney about your specific history before drawing any conclusions.

## The Payment Timeline: Patience Is Not Optional

Here's something that could catch people off guard. Of the full $7.35 billion, only $50 million is scheduled for 2026. Nothing is due in 2027. The bulk of the money flows from 2028 through 2034, with roughly 20% of the total concentrated in the final two years of that schedule.

What that means practically: even claimants who qualify and opt in shouldn't expect a check anytime soon. The present value of the settlement is approximately $5.8 billion, which reflects the time value of money across those eight years. Settlement administrators will work through the claims process, and individual payment amounts will vary based on severity of injury, duration and intensity of exposure, and other case-specific factors. No one should count on a fixed number right now.

The back-loaded structure also creates financial planning considerations for seriously ill plaintiffs. If you or a family member has an active cancer diagnosis and significant medical expenses now, the gap between today and when payments actually arrive could be meaningful. This is exactly the kind of situation where a conversation with both an attorney and a financial advisor is worth having.

## What About DuPont, Chemours, and Corteva?

This is where many people get confused. 3M's settlement is big news, but 3M was not the only manufacturer of AFFF. DuPont, Chemours, and Corteva are still named defendants in active litigation, and as of this writing, none of them have settled the personal injury claims against them.

The MDL litigation continues. According to MDL Update's November 2026 tracking of the South Carolina proceedings, Group B bellwether trial proposals covering thyroid disease and ulcerative colitis claims against remaining defendants were due November 20, 2026. Bellwether trials are test cases, a small selection of representative lawsuits tried first so both sides can gauge how juries respond to the evidence. They often drive settlement negotiations for the broader group of claims.

If your AFFF exposure involved foam manufactured by companies other than 3M, or if your exposure came from multiple sources, your legal options may extend well beyond this settlement. This is not a situation where one deal closes the book on all claims.

## The $680 Million Reserve and Future Claims

Buried in the structure of this settlement is a detail worth knowing: 3M set aside a $680 million reserve specifically for personal injury claims not covered by the main settlement pool, including future claims. As Motley Rice noted in their November 4, 2026 coverage of the deal, this reserve signals that 3M anticipated not every claim would fit neatly into the primary structure.

That reserve matters for a few groups. People whose diagnoses come after the settlement's cutoff dates, claimants with conditions not on the six-injury list, and those whose claims fall outside the covered litigation scope may have a path through that reserve fund rather than the main settlement. It's a narrower path with its own requirements, but it exists.

If you believe you have an AFFF-related injury that doesn't fit the six qualifying conditions, or if your diagnosis came recently, don't assume you're automatically excluded. The situation is still developing, and the claims process for the reserve fund hasn't been fully detailed publicly yet.

## What You Should Actually Do Right Now

The opt-in process is in motion. Deadlines exist. If you believe you're a potential claimant, the worst thing you can do is wait and assume someone will contact you. Mass-tort settlements like this one require active participation. You need to file a claim, provide documentation of your exposure history and diagnosis, and in most cases work through an attorney who handles these cases.

If you already have an attorney representing you in the AFFF MDL, they should be reaching out to you about your options under this settlement. If you don't have representation yet, consulting with a mass-tort attorney sooner rather than later makes sense, not because the deadlines for every individual payment are imminent, but because building your documentation file, medical records, employment history, and exposure evidence takes time.

You can also check the 3M News Center announcement from November 3, 2026, and the MDL Update tracker for the South Carolina proceedings to get current information directly from primary sources. Nothing in this article is legal advice, and given the complexity and financial stakes here, professional legal consultation isn't just advisable, it's worth prioritizing.

The 3M settlement is a major step, but it's one piece of a larger legal picture that's still being written.
