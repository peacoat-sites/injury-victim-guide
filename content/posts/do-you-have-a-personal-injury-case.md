If you've been hurt in an accident, one of the first questions on your mind is probably: *do I actually have a case?*

The short answer is: it depends on whether someone else's negligence caused your injury. Here's what that means in plain terms.

## The Four Elements You Need

To have a valid personal injury claim, you generally need to prove four things:

1. **Duty of care**,  The other party had a legal obligation to act reasonably toward you. Drivers owe this to other motorists. Property owners owe it to visitors.
2. **Breach of duty**,  They failed to meet that obligation. Running a red light. Ignoring a spill in a store aisle.
3. **Causation**,  Their breach directly caused your injury.
4. **Damages**,  You suffered real harm: medical bills, lost income, pain and suffering.

## What Counts as "Negligence"?

Negligence doesn't require intent. The driver who hit you didn't have to *mean* to hurt you. They just had to act carelessly,  speeding, texting, failing to yield. That's enough.

## What to Do Right Now

- **Seek medical treatment immediately.** Even if you feel okay. Delayed symptoms are common, and a gap in treatment can hurt your claim later.
- **Document everything.** Photos of the scene, your injuries, any property damage.
- **Don't give recorded statements** to insurance adjusters until you understand your rights.
- **Keep all receipts and records** related to your injury.

Most personal injury attorneys offer free consultations and work on contingency,  meaning they only get paid if you win. There's no cost to get a professional opinion on whether your situation qualifies.
