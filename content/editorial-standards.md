# How Injury Victim Guide Is Written

Injury Victim Guide explains what happens after an accident or injury: insurance claims, settlement values, filing deadlines, medical documentation and working with a lawyer. This page explains, plainly, how the articles are made.

## How articles are made

Articles on Injury Victim Guide are drafted with AI writing tools. Each draft starts from public reference sources on the topic, including those listed below, and from recent news when a topic is changing.

Before an article is published, a second automated pass reviews it and removes or rewrites anything it cannot stand behind: statistics, prices or dates it is not confident are correct, studies or organizations that may be misattributed, and personal stories presented as if they happened to the writer.

Articles are not reviewed by an attorney before publication. Use them as background for a conversation with one, not as a substitute.

The site owner audits published articles in batches, merging pages that overlap, deleting weak ones and correcting errors.

## Who writes here

The bylines on Injury Victim Guide (Denise Wallace, Jennifer Harris, Lisa Anderson, Maya Rivera, Rachel Thompson) are pen names, one for each area of coverage. They are not real individuals, they hold no professional licenses, and their profile images are illustrations, not photographs of real people.

## Sources we rely on

- [National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA)](https://www.nhtsa.gov/): Federal crash and vehicle-safety data
- [CDC Injury Center](https://www.cdc.gov/injury/): Injury prevention and statistics
- [American Bar Association](https://www.americanbar.org/): Consumer legal information
- [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/): Claims and insurance data

Where an article depends on a specific figure, rule or study, it links to or names the source so you can check it.

## Limits of this site

This is general legal information, not legal advice, and reading it does not create an attorney-client relationship. Deadlines to file a claim or lawsuit (statutes of limitations) vary by state and claim type and can be short, so talk to a licensed attorney in your state early. Many personal injury lawyers offer a free first consultation, and legal aid organizations help people with low incomes.

## Corrections

If you find an error, please report it through the [contact page](/contact/). Reports go directly to the site owner, and confirmed errors are corrected in the article.

## How the site makes money

Injury Victim Guide may earn money from display advertising and from affiliate links, including Amazon links (as an Amazon Associate, the site earns from qualifying purchases). Advertisers and affiliate partners do not see or approve articles before they are published.
